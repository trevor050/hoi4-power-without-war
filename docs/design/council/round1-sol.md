# Power Without War — Round 1 review by sol

## 1. Problems and fixes

### 1. Give the country under pressure a winning move (§§6.11–6.15)

The coercer gets an escalating toolkit while the target mostly gets an acceptance roll. That rewards a major for piling on every source of pressure and leaves a resource-rich minor waiting for its turn to lose. Make each crisis about **one demand and one deadline**, with a public or private channel and a named reason the target can still hold out. A private approach can leave the target room to offer a smaller concession. Going public makes a threat more costly to walk back and gives the target a reason to rally or call a patron.

Give the target time for one significant counterplay before the deadline. It can seek a replacement buyer, request limited protection, or offer one concession instead of a wider lease. For example, a pressured Portugal can sell output from one tungsten state for a year, ask Britain to underwrite a refusal, or announce a temporary suspension of exports to both rivals. Each changes what the coercer can win. Tie response cues to concrete outcomes. “Strained. London has promised to buy the unused output” tells the player why more ships might accomplish little. A refusal should offer a settlement or a visible decision to escalate. Reserve AI declarations of war for targets with a military plan and a credible expectation of support or survival.

### 2. Put a dependable bargain at the center of the trade empire (§§4, 6.3–6.7, 6.9, 6.14)

A promise of preferred buyers at variable net margins, exact output shares, and single-resource cutoffs rides on hooks whose delivery and cancellation semantics are open. The player needs to know what a signature will change in the production screen. Keep vanilla auto-import as the ordinary lane. Deliver the distinctive terms through a small set of named agreements with a verifiable payoff. Start with a named-state resource lease for equipment in installments and a non-committal agreement around an observed import if exporter-level delivery is measurable. Put contract value, scarcity, and routing risk into which agreements appear and what partners ask for.

If native imports cannot be locked, sell a cancellable supply arrangement and stop its side payments when verified delivery stops. If exact shares cannot be enforced, offer a small, medium, or large package of named producing states and show the expected units, with output changing naturally. For targeted cutoffs, begin with an ultimatum tied to a mod agreement the player can actually suspend. For wider pressure, use a separate **sea interdiction** action that can interrupt exposed mod-backed agreements while the local force stays on station. Call it a native blockade only when the vanilla diplomatic action is actually in effect. A protected or alternate route keeps the agreement valuable and gives the target a response.

### 3. Make default a political decision instead of a payment exploit (§§6.1, 6.4, 6.6, 6.10–6.11)

Upfront unlocks combined with a war exit that always forgives outstanding obligations make a short-lived deal a way to collect permanent technology or ships. A penalty worth less than the prize simply becomes its price. Settle irreversible transfers in tranches. A partner might release a first equipment batch when the lease takes effect, another after a quarter of service, and a limited research bonus at a later milestone. Limit the initial tranche to the value both sides can deliver now. Fully unlocked technology and named ship transfers belong in exceptional settlements with explicit prerequisites and delivery checks. Any matched PP transfer should debit the actual payer and credit the same amount, with a missed-payment grace period if they cannot pay.

Have the agreement list its own exit terms before signing. A **suspension** pauses deliveries and payments, with a dated opportunity to replace the route or reduce volume. A third-party interruption or involuntary war can produce a clean termination after that window. A party that starts a war to walk away or deliberately blocks its own delivery loses reliability and its remaining tranches. For nationalization, offer an actual settlement to the aggrieved party, such as staged equipment delivery or completion of a named project at the host's expense. Locking the host's factories on its own has no payoff for the aggrieved player. Carry disputes forward once so a failed agreement cannot mint repeated penalties or war goals.

### 4. Let a neutral country finish an economic campaign (§§1, 3, 6.8, 6.10, 10)

The design explains how to accumulate power but leaves neutral victory undefined. If permanent control is principally acquired by conquest or puppeting, leases may feel like temporary preparation for the familiar campaign. Add optional milestones in the Trade Desk with objectives visible from the start. A neutral broker could commit to keeping two rival markets supplied through a supply crisis, building a second route, and renewing a major lease without entering the war. A trade empire could instead hold a leading position in one resource, survive a coordinated challenge to its concessions, and get two customers to renew. Award a campaign milestone and a short account of the bargains and crises that earned it. The campaign continues, and the achievement needs no global cash score or economic buff.

### 5. Bring one complete story forward into the first playable phase (§§6.5–6.8, 7, 10)

The current sequencing delays contracts, an active rival, and the auction until after much of the coercion toolkit. That gives the team a pressure meter to tune without much reason to care about it. Build one complete tungsten story after the engine spike. Portugal can entertain two buyers, sign a one-year lease on a named state for equipment, and respond when the losing patron asks it to reconsider. End the story with a renewal, settlement, or loss. Play as Portugal and as the demanding patron before expanding the catalog. Run one pass without a great-power war so the stakes work during the 1936 opening.

Use one Trade Desk with an inbox row that opens the terms and relevant dossier. Surface a single actionable alert for the crisis, with its last response date. Replace the major-power popup rule with a consequential-choice rule. A routine British offer can sit in the inbox, while a minor's impending seizure of the player's only producing lease warrants an interruption. Bids and breach notices can update the same row so one story does not generate five alerts.

## 2. Things not considered

- **Dependence should run both ways (§§6.8, 6.13–6.15).** Count sustained, meaningful purchases relative to the seller's viable outlets, and count the buyer's reliance on that supply separately. Sample observable deliveries and signed flows, using a broad reliance estimate when bilateral vanilla data is missing. If a tungsten cartel blacklists a major customer, the cartel risks losing business and giving that customer a reason to finance a competing mine. Let secondary sanctions nominate named partners and carry a deadline. Gain credibility once per dispute by honoring a material commitment. Trade with a disposable puppet or a chain of token contracts earns little. A real breach of an existing trade commitment can cost reliability even when it earns credibility.
- **Protect renewals from double sales and stale rights (§§6.4, 6.8, 6.10).** A lease of a producing state locks its covered resource before an offer can become binding. Revalidate on acceptance and completion. The removal hook is documented at state granularity, so begin with one mod-managed grant per holder and state, and skip states where removing it would risk touching a vanilla grant. Register occupation, ownership changes, civil war, and vanilla nationalization as changes to reconcile. Occupation can pause a deal. A successor that actually owns the state can opt to honor it or take the opportunity to renegotiate. An empty mine pauses eligible delivery without erasing the agreement's history.
- **Avoid a proxy for shortage that rewards cargo cult imports (§§6.2, 6.16).** A lumpy deficit can be deliberate, a line can be lightly penalized, and oil stock on hand may buy the AI time. Consider deficit relative to consumption, duration, fuel runway, and the opportunity cost of civilian factories. Have the AI seek one worthwhile alternative before escalation. Reroute, substitute an equipment purchase, or offer a short contract if the issue is actually urgent. Track whether its resource balance or fuel outlook improved before it renews. A supplier AI should also retain enough for its own current commitments and wartime priorities.
- **Make deployment a choice with an opportunity cost (§§6.12–6.14).** One fleet should not boost five ultimatums from the same sea tile. Assign a demonstrator to one active crisis for a minimum time, require sustained eligible presence, and cap how much force alone moves the target. If local wing detection fails, use a time-limited mobilization decision gated by air force size and an eligible neighboring or partner airfield. Charge command power and describe it as an announced show of force. A protector's counter-demonstration or a viable replacement route can break the pressure without either side firing.
- **Treat the host's acceptance as part of construction (§6.9).** Reserve the investor's own civilian factories while a project runs and deliver the physical upgrade in its host state after an ownership and consent check. Stage big projects so an occupation stops the unfinished work and leaves completed buildings on the map. Make the commercial benefit specific to a named corridor. If a new railway leaves vanilla import routing unchanged, let the corridor keep an eligible agreement operating during sea interdiction or lower its agreed logistics burden. Describe that benefit in the contract, and leave unrelated sea imports on their original routes.
- **Set multiplayer and simulation rules before chasing balance (§§6.5–6.8, 6.16, 7, 9).** Give each human counterparty its own responses and deadlines in game time. Before applying a response, recheck the agreement ID, recipient, latest revision, and availability. Apply the result once, so simultaneous acceptances or clicking twice cannot award two leases. Store only active deals, crises, and recent counterparties. Stagger country shortage checks, evaluate a short list of counterparties only when a country has a reason to act, and update the GUI when visible state changes. Test two humans saving and resuming a live auction, and compare late-game tick time with the same hands-off save running without the mod.

## 3. New ideas

- **Let the seller write the auction terms.** Offer exclusive supply for a better deal, a split award that reduces dependence on either buyer, or a short bridge contract while the seller seeks a patron. Each buyer bids once on a named lot with an equipment tranche, project commitment, or promise to buy surplus. Reserve lots on award and use rival reactions to distinguish violating an exclusivity promise from merely picking a competitor. British procurement talks over Turkish chromium explicitly considered buying the output above Germany's existing commitment, a useful precedent for the split award. [U.S. diplomatic record, November 1942](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1942v04/d858)
- **Make protection a commercial counteroffer.** As a protector, offer to replace lost imports or buy a pressured country's unsold product in exchange for a shorter exclusive lease or a narrow policy commitment. The patron now risks its own factories or equipment and can get dragged into a later dispute if it reneges. Build it as a third-party offer on the existing crisis with a named, reserved agreement that activates only if the target refuses the demand. U.S. officials discussed replacing German goods and buying other Portuguese exports while seeking to restrict wolfram shipments. [U.S. diplomatic record, January 1944](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1944v04/d90)
- **Offer a temporary route closure with a diplomatic job to do.** Let the target agree to suspend covered deliveries for 90 days, give both sides a chance to negotiate a longer settlement, and restore deliveries by default on the known date. Build it as one timed clause on a defined route, backed by a vanilla hook where available and by pausing named agreements otherwise. The reopening is a second playable decision, especially if the threatened country has spent the interval finding support. Britain used a three-month Burma Road agreement and decided to reopen the route at expiry after prospects for a settlement faded. [British decision relayed to Washington, October 1940](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1940v04/d195)

## 5. Your top 5

1. Write and play the tungsten crisis from both seats, including a credible way for the target to prevail.
2. Prove one enforceable named-state lease, with a real production effect, reversible cleanup, and checks for what vanilla trade actually delivers.
3. Build one shared agreement lifecycle for staged payment, suspension, default, nationalization, and renewal.
4. Ship one Trade Desk that shows the deal, the deadline, and the reasons a counterparty might change course.
5. Define neutral campaign milestones and play them through a prewar opening and a wartime supply shock before tuning the global market.
