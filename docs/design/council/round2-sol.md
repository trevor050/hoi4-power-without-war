# Power Without War — Round 2 review by sol

## 1. The core loop is securing an alternative before you need it

Build the Trade Desk around Opus's Situations, with Neon’s patron intervention as the reason a situation stays interesting after the first offer. Each situation starts with an asset, a decision date, and a credible alternative the player can improve. A mine brings in equipment today. A competing buyer gives its owner a way to refuse a bad renewal next year. A second route makes a threatened cutoff survivable. The player spends the proceeds of the first deal building the option that wins the next one.

The loop is **spot a contested supply → choose what to commit and whom to rely on → sign or walk → use the proceeds to improve your position → face the next challenge with the commitments you made**. As a buyer, a short, expensive bargain gets production moving while you cultivate another source. As a neutral seller, a longer exclusive deal finances more development but gives the buyer a reason to lean on you at renewal. A quiet stretch should give you one useful decision about the next delivery or expiry. Let the player set that policy once and advance time.

Give the Desk one row per live situation, with the outcome at stake, the decision date and the latest development. Open the row to see the relevant terms and dossier. Let the dossier start an inquiry even when the board is quiet. Keep the initial board to two live choices that need player attention, while existing agreements continue to run. Pin an alert when delay would close a consequential option. A routine offer from London can wait in the Desk. A deadline to save the only lease feeding your production line deserves the alert.

## 2. Give each promise an asset and each threat an exit

**Use a native state lease for the first exclusive deal.** Keep the contract record authoritative for who agreed to what and when it expires. Let a single verified engine path deliver the supply. Start by leasing the tungsten from one named state for staged equipment payments. Use a resource-filtered native right if it passes the supply and cleanup checks below. That concession is easy to understand on the map, gives the host something concrete to reclaim, and has a good chance of reserving real production before the open market allocates it.

I now favor Opus and Neon’s independently controlled flows for flexible quotas once the accounting works. I would decline either proposed modifier pairing as the default until the seller's *tradable* supply is tested. An added resource on the buyer and a cost on the seller could improve the buyer's balance while simply making the seller consume more, leaving some of its exports available for a second sale. A signed deal should move the source lot out of the pool from which it was offered. Reserve that lot once, in the same acceptance that rechecks the owner, availability and terms. Test actual output, export availability and production on both sides under different export laws and auto-import. If native rights fail and the modifier path cannot prove a source debit, try paired, reversible state production adjustments with tracked ownership and expiry. Use another verified lot or delivery path until the original one works.

**Make market power a choice at renewal.** Neon’s rationing idea works best as an auction choice over the output the player can actually reserve. Keep the ordinary surplus on automatic trade. Offer three meaningful terms for the controlled lot. An exclusive buyer pays more for a longer commitment. A split award gives two buyers smaller lots and keeps both interested in renewing. A patron can underwrite a short bridge deal while a longer contract is contested. Underwriting commits the patron to a named purchase or equipment shipment if the threatened buyer walks. This gives preclusive buying teeth: the rival can identify the supply it lost, while the patron pays for the commitment whether it needs the tungsten or not. If speculators later get reserves, track actual diverted deliveries and spend them down when used. A fixed stock of stored output and a time-limited production bonus need matching units and an explicit conversion.

**Use the price conversation to find a deal both sides can live with.** Keep exact quantities, equipment models, shipment dates and exit terms visible. Hide valuation, willingness and patience. A counter changes a commercial choice, such as replacing a large immediate rifle tranche with two deliveries or offering Lisbon instead of Guarda. Reveal one concrete red line or want per reply. A repeated, equivalent proposal gets the same response until the world changes. My round 1 review leaned on three counter rounds as a guardrail. I now see that the useful limit is the deadline and the number of materially different terms on the table. Three is a reasonable ceiling. A deadline should still bite if the player spends every round searching for a better offer.

## 3. Make a third party pay to change the answer

Play the crisis over the same lot and agreement. A **quiet demand** offers a narrow settlement and leaves room for a face-saving revision. A **public ultimatum** stakes credibility on a specific exclusion or concession and opens the door to an outside bid. Either can win substantial terms if the target's alternatives are poor. Public pressure can make even a small demand harder to accept once refusal has domestic backing. Let regime matter through what the government can publicly defend and what it privately needs, with the dossier naming that constraint.

Give the target time to play one substantive response before the decision date. It can offer another lot, make a visible commitment to keep selling to both sides, or ask an interested patron to make good on an alternative. Give the patron one intervention with something at risk. London could commit a support-equipment shipment in return for a short, non-exclusive lease if Berlin withdraws its offer. London gains access and keeps Lisbon free to sell again at renewal. Portugal gives up output and accepts a new dependence. Berlin can accept a smaller allocation, honor its stated cutoff, or back down and lose credibility with the countries that saw it commit. A patron that fails to deliver gives Lisbon a claim and loses reliability. A warning without an offer can signal intent, with its effect limited by what the target believes that patron will actually do.

This also makes “Wavering” and the war warning independent. Lisbon can be looking for a deal while mobilizing to refuse the demand as written. Show the movement and its cause. “Britain guaranteed a replacement shipment. Lisbon can now refuse your cutoff.” Keep the material change and deadline visible at any intel level. Better civilian intel can reveal the cabinet’s red line or confidence in the patron. Resolve a missed deadline according to the offer that was actually on the table, then allow a deliberate escalation if the coercer still has the means and the political appetite. Mere expiry or refusal should never fire a war automatically.

A later **quarantine** should put a named sea agreement in jeopardy while a demonstrator commits real forces to that approach. A patron can help contest the posture or fund another route. Quarantine pauses the exposed portion and the corresponding payment after sustained pressure. It opens a repair window for partial delivery, temporary protection or a corridor already built with host consent. For a nationalization, use the same dispute structure. Where a focus or occupation interrupts a right, reconcile control and production before assigning blame. A hostile new government can honor it in return for a shorter term, buy it out in equipment, or openly seize it. An empty mine pauses delivery. A third party's interference opens a grievance. A party that engineers its own interruption forfeits unpaid benefits and loses standing for the breach. Pay out only for completed service so the dispute has something to settle without allowing a player to pocket the full return up front.

## 4. Where I changed my mind and where I differ

- **My observed-import contract can wait.** The documented resource totals give the dossier good shortage context. They cannot establish who kept a bilateral promise. An at-will agreement can use the same real reservation as the first lease, with an explicit release date and flows that stop together. Bring native imports into enforceable terms only if bilateral delivery becomes observable. I would likewise build secondary sanctions around named demands to join an embargo or close a known mod route. Verify the commitment the third party actually made. Reputation should follow observable action, including the cost of following through, rather than inferred secret trading.
- **Opus’s native diplomacy hooks are a way in.** I would give the shared situation record control of the clock, revisions, rivals and contingent offers. A two-party action can open it or transmit a response if those callbacks survive save/reload and the target playset. It cannot by itself decide which of two bids owns a lot and whether a protector’s promise funds the winning response. Permit AI offers only when the seller has capacity and the bidder has a specific reason to act. A supply loss, upcoming expiry or patron’s commitment is enough to wake up a short list of relevant countries. Give the GUI one decision-category entry and try Neon’s top-bar shortcut as an additive attachment. Use a dirty variable and test the actual playset for collisions. A later map mode earns its place by showing where a route, owned right or actionable alternative is located.
- **An equipment purchase is already a paid transaction.** Opus’s `create_purchase_contract` suggestion brings a useful equipment market into reach, but its documented example charges civilian factories. Test whether it can express the intended payment before using it for barter. Start with stockpile-backed equipment tranches and verify the debit and receipt. For clearing agreements, take the historical dependency and attach it to a named resupply option. The arms supplier can commit to a later batch of spares in return for renewal priority. That keeps Neon’s choice to rely on one equipment ecosystem and avoids a standing balance of blocked credit. Introduce market subsidies later only if the cost to the backer and scope of redemption are clear.
- **Use a domestic cost for offensive coercion in place of a blanket regime gate.** Neon is right that a major needs a reason to pass on an easy target. Put one substantial offshore demonstration in its budget and make public escalation against a neutral consume political support and invite a material patron bid. A fresh grievance makes public escalation easier to defend, and an unprovoked grab is still possible at a price. Test this by giving a 1936 UK player two tempting targets at once. Protecting an existing route or buying a voluntary concession should sometimes beat another shakedown.
- **Make economic victory describe what the player survived.** A global share threshold may reward grabbing the last easy mine while a neutral broker’s best game is keeping two blocs interested without becoming captive to either. Offer a visible campaign objective to keep crucial supply moving through a wartime shock and reach a second renewal with a credible alternative. A concession empire can instead survive a coordinated challenge and renew with multiple hosts. Spend the returns on the alternate route, host investment or tangible patron commitment that gets you there. Give the finish a short account of the bargains that kept the country independent or secured its supply. Include coal and `energy_ratio` in the resource schema from the start, then use coal as the next campaign to make the energy consequences matter in play.

## 5. First playable slice

Build **one Portugal tungsten story**, playable from Portugal, Britain and Germany. Start with a live offer in a prewar save. Use Guarda and Lisbon as the first candidate lots, validating their control and availability in vanilla and Road to 56. Portugal can lease Guarda for more equipment on exclusive terms, or keep it uncommitted while offering Lisbon on a shorter deal. The disappointed buyer makes one demand that names the supply at risk. The other buyer can back a refusal with a concrete shipment and term. One dated renewal then tests whether Portugal bought itself freedom of action or simply changed patrons. Put the short deal, exclusive deal and refusal in the same interface with the same assets and clocks. Test the story once with a wartime supply shock too.

The first slice needs one verified resource delivery path, equipment in tranches, an actionable demand and patron counteroffer, and one expiry or early interruption. A live contract cutoff is enough to test whether coercion bites. Add the physical sea route next, with control and duration checks, and prove that a protector or completed alternate route really restores delivery. That sequence lets the team play the political loop before solving every logistics unknown, while giving the maritime fantasy a near-term acceptance test.

**Call the slice playable when these checks hold.**

1. From Portugal, two commercially sensible paths produce different bargaining positions at renewal. The host can refuse a major and come out ahead by sacrificing something real.
2. From the demanding buyer, keeping a smaller supply on tolerable terms can beat doubling down, and an outright success changes the buyer’s production screen.
3. The patron’s offer changes the target’s choices, costs the patron what it promised and remains valid on save/reload. The player can tell which commitment changed the outcome without seeing any score.
4. Supply and equipment reconcile at signing, each tranche and expiry. Save/reload a pending bid, try simultaneous or stale acceptances, and interrupt a live contract by occupation or cancelled delivery. Check that no lot, shipment or grievance is awarded twice and no unrelated right is removed. Enable auto-import on the affected buyers and compare actual balances, export availability and production with the same save before the agreement.
5. Run the same story in vanilla and the target Road to 56 playset. Check one dusk-to-deadline AI-vs-AI run and one human-as-target run for response timing and alert noise. Check the Desk and route-entry placement in the actual UI setup.
